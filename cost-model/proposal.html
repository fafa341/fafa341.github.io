The Knowledge Problem
A working notebook

How can a daily sketch reveal what a product really costs?

A manufacturer of custom products priced everything the same way for thirty years. They lost track of how to price products and the answer to what it actually cost was already on the paper — drawn by hand, every morning. This is the notebook of how we read it.

↳ the answer was on the paper the whole time
By Fabio Fuentes Field Report 10 min read

Every new order at this company starts as a sketch. Measurements and a language that only the operators know. That sketch tells the entire cost of a product — which materials, which processes it will go through and for how much time. If you know how to read it.

What follows is the working notebook: how we didn't introduce new technology to the way they operate, but how we taught the computer to read their language — and how this reveals the true costs of the business, lets them know how to optimize, where to place their bets, and price competitively.

A story on how fixing structure can give you an edge, and how that matters for a solid growth plan.

Plate 00
The ideaLanguage · Structure
The loop

Knowledge hides where nobody is looking. Experts bring it to the surface every day. Each organization speaks their own language. We just need to give it structure, make it consistent through time, and repeat the loop.

The machine identifies the symbols, the dimensions, and rewrites them in the company's own language.

So we can create a closed loop around it to store and transmit knowledge. Improving learning and execution — enhancing how we humans learn, theorize, and execute.

Proper defining of what means what was crucial. Deciding what counts as a "part," a "route," a "difficulty," and shaping it so it fit the language the company already used. Read the drawing in their terms, or the number means nothing.

Act One
The problemMany businesses don't
know their exact costs

Many businesses don't know their exact costs.

Companies that make custom work struggle with this. Products like custom kitchens, trash bins, counters, range hoods, shelves. Everything is different — sizes, shapes, amount of work, different operators. No two orders are quite alike.

For years they price to cover costs with hard intuition. It covers costs, makes them profitable, that works, and nobody questions it.

What it hides: pricing competitively knowing real margin per product, effort per product, optimization alternatives, and prioritization.

The problem shows when you try to look closely. When you try to price products without production knowledge. Salespeople misprice, lose deals, win unprofitable deals, don't know how to prioritize, margins are hidden — where do you place your bets?

The only reason it hadn't become a crisis was volume and luck. That is not a plan.

Even though every product is custom, they don't all take a unique path through the factory. They share routes. A cylindrical bin and a custom bucket have different dimensions, but they both go through cutting, rolling, welding, and polishing. The route is the same. Only the difficulty changes.

Think of it as a product's manufacturing DNA. Two things that look nothing alike can share the same DNA if they pass through the same steps. We sorted all thirteen hundred products this way — not by what they look like, but by what happens to them on the floor.

The modelBreaking it down ·
Four drivers

The root cause is never the formula. It's the breakdown.

The root cause isn't the multiplier. It's that breaking down what everything actually costs to make a specific custom product is genuinely hard — if you're not the one building it. There are no automatic robots, just humans. Repetition across multiple years lets you understand, and if you're not on the production floor you won't.

So you keep breaking it down. Knowing the route isn't enough. You also need to know how hard that route is for this specific product. A small simple bin and a big complex one both get rolled — but they're nowhere near the same amount of work.

Act Three
The hard partKnowledge in
one head

Here the story gets harder. The data is not in a spreadsheet anywhere.

None of this model works without the data inside it. And this data is not in a spreadsheet anywhere. It's in the heads of the people who actually operate — they've worked here for more than thirty years.

They know by instinct which processes are involved and how long each step takes. They know which products will be trouble before they even start. They can look at a sketch and feel the complexity. They've been doing it so long they don't even think about it consciously anymore.

It's real. It's accurate. And it's completely invisible to everyone else in the building.

Getting it out of his head was the hardest part of the whole project. You can't just sit someone down and say tell me everything you know. That's not how expertise works. It's contextual. The whole job was turning it depends into something concrete.

Every organisation has a Hernán.
Someone whose knowledge runs everything — without anyone realising.

And when that person leaves, they take all of it with them. The knowledge doesn't exist anywhere else. The organisation starts again from zero.

p. 05
Act Four
The methodDon't change it ·
Intercept it

Don't change the workflow. Intercept it.

But let's not take the "let's digitize the factory" approach — it fails the same way every time. Cameras with vision models? Filling spreadsheets? A microphone on the expert's shirt? Everything asks people to do new things: new forms, new habits that no one adopts. Friction becomes abandonment.

What if we think big and actually use the capabilities of current technology? This is a human-computer interaction problem. Find the thing that already carries the information you need, and wrap the technology around that.

The expert already draws a sketch for every order — and each operator adds to it as well. It carries all the information that we need. We just need to add a simple loop that barely touches him at all.

Act Five
The resultWhen the number
is right

What happens when the number is right.

Salespeople stop guessing. They can quote complex products with confidence — the system shows its work. This product takes this route, at this difficulty, and that's why it costs what it costs. A completely different conversation.

The company can finally see margin by product type. Which categories are underpriced, which have room, how to place bets on more complex products. This gives them a competitive edge — price sharper, hold the line when there's no room. Mix with imported cheaper parts. Discriminate costs as much as you can and deliver a great product.

Then you push hard on growth — when you can track everything.

New people don't need two years to absorb expert instinct by osmosis. The model is a baseline that explains its own logic. Build and quote complex projects in minutes, deliver a fast and confident experience.

The factory didn't change. Hernán still draws his sketches every morning — but now, when the sketch is done, the number is right.

p. 07
Closing
The bigger pointOne factory ·
Every organisation

This system pushes for change. Causes a market change. Experts still draw sketches every morning, but now when the sketch is done, everyone speaks the same economic language. You can intercept it with new tech — maybe new AI tools for sketching, new automatic robotic machines, anything. But now all language is formalized and variations are tracked.

This story is about one factory. The pattern is everywhere. Every organisation has knowledge that lives inside specific people and nowhere else. Knowledge is exactly what makes them good at what they do.

When those people leave, everything leaves with them.

Each organisation speaks its own language. The work is to formalize that language — structure it so a process can be built around it, stored and transmitted. A better way for an organisation to learn, theorize, and execute.

Knowledge hides where nobody is looking.

Experts surface it every day without realising. The job is to catch it before it disappears — the pricing engine is just the output.
